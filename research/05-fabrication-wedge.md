# 05 — Advanced fabrication wedge and who pays (2026-09-01)

Method: 23 searches by a research agent. Purpose: which part of a
"universal fabricator" to hatch first, and which customer funds it.

## Voxel-level multi-material printing
- Inkbit (MIT spinout, 2017): Vision-Controlled Jetting — scans every
  layer, corrects the next; jets thiol-enes, epoxies, true elastomers.
  ~$45M+ (Phoenix, Stratasys, 3M, DSM, Ocado, Saint-Gobain) + $19M
  (Ingersoll Rand). USAF bought three Vista systems ($1.7M SBIR).
  2025–26 pivot from machine sales to parts/materials service (TEPU
  elastomers via Stratasys Direct, Apr 2026).
- Quantica (Berlin): €19.7M Series A; NovoJet OPEN high-viscosity
  jetting (~250 mPa·s vs ~15 PolyJet).
- Stratasys PolyJet: ~49 digital materials, no conductive inks.
- Nano Dimension: after M&A chaos sold AME business to Inspira for $2M
  upfront (Sept 2025) — best-funded printed electronics effort
  liquidated.
- XJet (metal/ceramic jetting; Carmel Pro Q2 2026); BMF (2–25µm; 2–10µm
  dual-res 2026).
- Nobody co-prints structural + conductive + functional in one
  production machine. Closest: nScrypt/Optomec hybrid direct-write at
  prototype volumes; Inkbit has the architecture, not conductive inks.

## Closed-loop / AI process control
Feature, not yet a category. Inkbit only closed-loop-native. Metal:
Seurat ($99M C incl. NVentures; $178M total; 25 t/yr Boston pilot,
Siemens Energy 59 t deal, ~4,000 t LOIs); VulcanForms ($220M D Feb
2026, ~$566M total); Velo3D survived via defense (2025 revenue $46M,
$32.6M Project FORGE, $11.5M RPS Feb 2026). Software: 1000 Kelvin
(AMAIZE, ~$3M, in EOS suite), Phase3D, Addiguru; ASTRO America/ASTM
in-situ monitoring challenge = DoD paying to make monitoring
actionable. Monitoring→autonomous correction still mostly open-loop
flagging in metal AM.

## Functional-system printing
No "complete device in one machine" product. Demos: nScrypt/Stratasys
ULTEM quadcopter with embedded electronics; Optomec conformal circuits
in production. Sakuu retreated from battery printing (equipment
auctioned Aug 2025). Bioprinting first: Precise Bio corneal implant
transplanted Oct 29, 2025. Redwire monetizes in-space manufacturing as
pharma crystallization, not structures.

## Who pays (Wohlers 2026: $24.2B in 2025, +10.9%; services 48%)
1. Defense — DoD FY2026 AM request $3.3B (+83% YoY); USAF $46B EWAAC
   incl. Divergent, Elementum 3D; Divergent $290M E at $2.3B (rev 5x,
   600+ part numbers); Firestorm Labs ($160M total; $100M USAF IDIQ;
   $82M round Apr 2026) containerized drone factories; Ursa Major
   printed solid motors. Uniquely tolerates unqualified processes for
   attritable systems.
2. Medical/dental — dental 3D printing ~$4.9B (2025), implants ~$2.8–3B
   (+16%/yr); slow qualification, funds incumbents.
3. Space/energy — Seurat/Siemens Energy, Velo3D/iRocket.
4. Electronics prototyping — never reached production economics.

## The shakeout's lesson (2023–26)
Dead/restructured: Desktop Metal (Ch. 11 Jul 2025), Shapeways (Ch. 7
Jul 2024), Fast Radius, Forward AM, Zortrax, Voxeljet, Sintratec;
Markforged sold to Stratasys for $42.5M (vs $116M Nano paid). What died:
general-purpose machines into prototyping, SPAC growth stories,
roll-ups. What raises in 2026: parts services with proprietary process
advantage sold into defense.

## Verdict
Wedge that compounds toward universal fabrication: vision-in-the-loop
material jetting (chemistry-agnostic, voxel-addressable,
self-correcting). Customer that funds it first: US defense. Realistic
path: defense-funded multi-material + embedded-electronics parts
services 2026–2030.
