# 15 — Kill research I: Backflip traction forensics (2026-09-02)

Method: 24 search/fetch attempts (Similarweb, HN Algolia, Bing RSS,
DuckDuckGo, jina reader, pullpush (rate-limited), vendor sites, Reddit
mirrors, Autodesk marketplace). Mandate: try to KILL the idea.

## 1. Backflip traction: not there
- Similarweb: ~9,300 total visits over 3 months; down 60% MoM; global
  rank #2,351,292 and falling (was #1.17M three months earlier);
  16-second average visit; traffic led by Brazil (28%) and Vietnam
  (19%) — bot/press-spike signature. One month after the CAD Copilot
  launch.
- Community/social: HN launch story 1 point, 0 comments (Jan 2025);
  "scan to CAD" has zero HN stories in the API's history; no Discord,
  subreddit, or forum footprint; fintech Backflip.com drowns the brand;
  all press = same-day syndication of the Aug 4 release; zero
  independent hands-on reviews found (the skeptical engineering.com
  piece 404s a month later).
- Company signals: one open role; homepage has no customer logos,
  testimonials, or user counts — the metric is a price, not usage.
  Fusion add-in not findable on the Autodesk marketplace (possibly
  direct-distributed); no visible install counts/reviews.
- Pivot history: Dec 2024 text-to-3D for printing ($30M Series A
  announced simultaneously — raise preceded PMF evidence); Mar 2025
  shift to scan-to-3D-printable; Aug 2026 homepage scrubs text-to-3D.
  Two products in 20 months; the first left no user residue.

## 2. The 20-year question
QuickSurface (quicksurface3d.com) is a trademark of KVS Ltd, run by
Kostadin Vrantzaliev (also creator of Mesh2Surface), since ~2010,
contact = a single UK mobile number. 15+ years selling exactly this at
hobbyist-reachable prices and still a micro-business with zero HN
mentions. Geomagic barely registers in 25 years of HN. The affordable
niche has been continuously served and never grew anyone big; winners
sold enterprise seats at $10–30K and got acquired.

## 3. What scanner owners actually do — surprise counter-finding
r/3DScanning top posts this month: ~14 of 19 mechanical/reverse-
engineering (Toyota MR2 window switch, obsolete motorcycle toolbox lid,
tow bar, oil tank cap, Mercedes seat, process posts "Reverse
Engineering… what to do with mesh data when needing CAD"). Faces/
cosplay nearly absent. Revopoint's site leads with Reverse Engineering
as use case #1. Caveats: showcase bias, single month, pullpush
rate-limiting.

## 4. Frequency of need
The RE projects are one-off artifacts (one switch, one lid). Recurring
community question: "Is 3D scanning worth it for the average hobbyist
yet?" Mesh-cleanup pain ("80 GB file nightmare") suggests the binding
constraint is scan wrangling. Episodic need + free-ish alternatives =
vitamin pricing tolerance; QuickSurface sells perpetual licenses to a
trickle.

## Verdict
Bear (strongest): real but tiny and episodic market; 15-year-old
sub-$1K incumbent never outgrew one developer; Backflip with $30M,
star founders, Autodesk partnership → ~3K declining monthly visits, no
community, no hiring, one pivot already. If they can't find users, a
bootstrapper won't.
Bull (survives): use-case mix is genuinely RE-heavy; installed base
growing; one month post-launch is early and in-Fusion usage wouldn't
show as web traffic; Backflip's failure may be distribution/brand;
enterprise RE lane ($1,500/part) verifiably real — hobbyist lane
unproven.
What would settle it: Backflip Fusion add-in install/rating counts at 6
months; KVS Ltd Companies House filings; a 12-month r/3DScanning census
of RE posts per unique user per year; Backflip's next raise/bridge/
sunset by mid-2027.
Net: lean kill for a hobbyist-first standalone product.
