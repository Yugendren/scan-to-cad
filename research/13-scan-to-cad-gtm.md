# 13 — Monetization & go-to-market for scan-to-CAD (2026-09-02)

Method: ~14 direct fetches of pricing/product pages + prior knowledge
(search quota exhausted). YouTube subscriber counts, Kickstarter
figures, subreddit sizes are order-of-magnitude.

## Pricing benchmarks (fetched Sept 2026)
Backflip: free 60 credits one-time; Builder $20/mo (100); Pro $50
(300); Business $380 (2,500); no rollover; ~$2–10 per serious job.
Polycam: free; Basic $150/yr ($30 monthly); Business $400/yr/user.
Shapr3D: free 2-project tier; Pro ~$299/yr. Adam: Free/Pro/Max/Team
(historically $6–18/mo). Zoo: free + Pro unlimited Design Studio,
metered API, no charge for failed calls. HueForge (key comp): $24
one-time Personal lifetime (reg. $30) with 2 yrs updates; tiered
Limited/Professional Commercial licenses gating what you may SELL; paid
plugins; bundled filament coupons. Pro RE anchors: QuickSurface/
Mesh2Surface one-time (~$490–3,000); Design X five figures; Fusion
~$680/yr with free hobbyist tier.
Community tolerance: subscription-fatigued (Bambu cloud controversies,
Chitubox Pro grumbling) but HueForge proved one-time $20–40 works and
makers who sell prints accept annual commercial licenses; Backflip
proves credits accepted for compute-heavy jobs.

## Recommendation — three rails
- Hobbyist: $29–49 one-time "personal, local-first" (2 yrs updates,
  watermark-free STL/STEP, N cloud conversions/mo if any).
- Pro/commercial: $15–25/mo or ~$180–250/yr (undercut Shapr3D/Polycam
  Business; Backflip Builder level) — commercial use, batch,
  accuracy reports, priority.
- Credits/metered for heavy compute (10/50 fast/thinking pattern);
  Zoo-style API rail later; never charge failed jobs.

## Channels, ranked
1. YouTube scanner/printing reviewers (highest ROI): "can I actually
   get a usable model?" is the #1 pain in every scanner review.
   Maker's Muse (~1.2M), 3D Printing Nerd (~850k), Uncle Jessy (~600k,
   reviews every Revopoint/Creality), Teaching Tech (~500k), Thomas
   Sanladerer (~500k), My Tech Fun (~350k, accuracy tests), CNC
   Kitchen (~1M, accuracy-obsessed).
2. Reddit/forums: r/3DScanning (~60–80k), r/functionalprint,
   r/Machinists, r/hobbycnc, Revopoint/Creality forums.
3. Facebook scanner groups + Discords (post-purchase frustration).
4. Building in public (HueForge, Obico grew via Discord/devlogs).
5. PH/HN: one-day amplifier.

## Partnerships
Scanner OEMs are the prize: Revopoint/Creality/3DMakerpro have
affiliate programs, weak downstream software, no app stores → bizdev
bundle deals ("free 3-month Pro for registrants"); Kickstarter
audiences large (Revopoint POP2 ~13k backers/$4.6M; MIRACO ~$2M+) — a
campaign bundle slot = 5–10k-user event. Shining3D lower priority.
FreeCAD addon manager: no payment rails, community hostile to
paywalled cores → free companion importer funnel. Autodesk marketplace:
paid apps allowed, poor discovery.

## Adjacent revenue & sequencing
Bureaus $250–2,000+/part → profitable $79–199/part semi-automated
service. Evidence favors service-first, product-soon: validates WTP,
generates the messy-scan corpus, funds development. Cap at ~20% time
post-ship; convert repeat customers to Pro. API licensing to OEMs
("one-click CAD" in capture apps) is the endgame (Zoo metered-API
template).

## Three launch plays
1. "Accuracy Gauntlet" reviewer launch: free lifetime Pro + benchmark
   kit (known-geometry part → scan → CAD → deviation report) to My Tech
   Fun, Uncle Jessy, CNC Kitchen, Teaching Tech; one-week embargo; $29
   launch price; free web demo.
2. OEM Kickstarter piggyback: 3 months Pro for backers; weekly free
   "fix my scan" thread in r/3DScanning.
3. Open companion, paid core: free open-source FreeCAD/Fusion import +
   mesh-prep plugin funneling to the paid local-first app; PH/HN story
   "your noisy $500 scan, editable, locally, no subscription."
